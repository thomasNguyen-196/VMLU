## Context

See `proposal.md` for motivation. `diagnostic-protocol-ledger-pilot` froze the 60 anchors (MC-72 source, seed 42, 12 per dataset, 15 blind-human keys) and the readiness gates; its `inference_ready` requires `audit_complete` plus every prospective control in `data/diagnostic_v1/protocol.json`, all of which are unset. The SHA-256 of that file binds the review page, `import-review` and adjudication (`_read_pilot`), the assistant-suggestion manifest and the browser and Mongo draft keys, so it must not change while the human review is in progress.

Existing machinery to reuse: the frozen main-v1 manifest (2,043 items, replicate id `main-000`, `EvalItem` records with prompt, gold and task), the frozen extractors and scorers in `vi_multimodel.py` and `score_vi_multimodel.py`, and the MC-80/82/83 direct-API engine (`execute`: lock file, condition freeze, shards by lanes workers, attempt ledger, resume). Two properties of the frozen scorers shape the design: `score_rows` groups summaries by dataset only and would therefore pool arms, and the multiple-choice parser takes the first key-phrase match, which misreads a response that discusses other options before its final answer.

Measured on 2026-10-22 (24 synthetic prompts, 3 workers, Responses API, temperature 1.0): the endpoint reports `/home/llm/llama.cpp/models/Qwen3.5-9B-Q4_K_M.gguf`, reports no reasoning tokens, answers B-style prompts in 2 to 7 tokens (mean latency 3 s) and S-style prompts in 140 to 970 tokens (mean latency 7 s); effective parallelism was 2.93 of 3. These numbers calibrate budgets only; they are not benchmark results.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:** a campaign whose every request is traceable to frozen hashes; arms that differ only in the declared way; automatic scoring wherever gold exists so effect sizes do not wait for humans; a held-out split; fail-closed gates; analysis code fixed before data.

**Non-Goals:** everything under Non-goals in the proposal; a human review UI for campaign outputs; any change to the frozen scorers or prompts.

## Decisions

### 1. Two phases on one engine
`core` is the 60 anchors (three replicates; since Decision 11 the set the router is evaluated on). `wide` is the other 1,239 items of the same five datasets (one replicate, arms B and S only; since Decision 11 the set the router is designed on). Alternative: one pooled manifest; rejected because replicate policy, gating (core needs `inference_ready`, wide needs only a registered card) and role (router design versus evaluation) differ.

Sizes: core = 60x2x3 = 360 (B, S) plus three replicates of O, K and KS on each evidence anchor of Decision 11 (at most 12x3x3 = 108 more; K and KS only if corpus v0 passes its checks); wide = 1,239x2x1 = 2,478 attempts (per dataset 134 Legal MC, 138 Legal NLI, 188 Vi-SQuAD, 188 Vi-DROP, 591 ViBidLQA). From the probe: core takes well under one hour and wide about one to four hours at 3 to 12 workers (serial worst case about 3.5 h); expected usage is about 2 M tokens in total.

### 2. Arm encoded in the replicate id
`replicate_id = <ARM>-r<k>` (for example `S-r2`), so run keys stay `dataset:item:replicate` and the engine, the ledger identity triple and the manifest equality check work unchanged; arm items are `EvalItem` copies made with `dataclasses.replace` (new replicate id and arm prompt). Alternative: one run directory per arm; rejected because arms would execute hours apart and endpoint drift would be confounded with arm. Items are ordered item-major (item, replicate, arm) and the engine's contiguous shard split plus lane striding keeps the attempts of one item adjacent in time. Summaries are produced by calling the frozen `score_rows` once per arm on an arm-filtered manifest.

### 3. Transport and sampling
Direct API on `http://llmapi.iec/v1`: OpenAI Responses API, streaming, `temperature=1.0`, no seed, no reasoning field, system prompt "Answer the user's question.", header `x-9router-token-saver: off`, SDK retries 0, 300 s timeout, 12 workers (4 shards x 3 lanes), `max_output_tokens=4096` for every arm and dataset (the largest observed S output was 966 tokens; 4,096 equals the historical multiple-choice cap). The shape is that of MC-83 so the engine adapter is reused, with the cap parameterised instead of the MC-82 module constant. Alternative: the OMP harness; rejected because it adds an agent scaffold as a confound and is slower.

### 4. Prompts
B is the frozen main-v1 prompt, byte-identical and verified by hash. S replaces the answer-only instruction by a decomposition instruction and a pinned final line and drops the trailing completion cue; the question, options or passage are untouched, so S adds no information. O and K prepend one frozen evidence block to the B prompt; KS prepends the same block to the S prompt. Construction is string surgery on the frozen prompt with head and tail assertions that fail closed. Template text lives in `data/diagnostic_prospective_v1/prompt_templates_v1.json` and its SHA-256 is part of the condition. Draft wording (Vietnamese, to be validated and then frozen):

- S, multiple choice: "Trả lời câu hỏi trắc nghiệm sau. Hãy phân rã bài toán thành các bước nhỏ, kiểm tra từng bước dựa trên thông tin đã cho trong đề, rồi kết luận. Dòng cuối cùng của câu trả lời phải đúng một dòng có dạng `Đáp án: X` (X là một chữ cái A, B, C, D hoặc E), không thêm ký tự hay định dạng nào khác."
- S, reading: "Đọc đoạn văn dưới đây và trả lời câu hỏi. Hãy phân rã câu hỏi thành các bước nhỏ, đối chiếu từng bước với đoạn văn, rồi kết luận. Dòng cuối cùng phải đúng một dòng có dạng `Trả lời: <cụm từ hoặc số ngắn gọn, lấy nguyên văn trong đoạn văn khi có thể>`, không dùng định dạng markdown và không viết thêm gì sau dòng đó."
- Evidence block: "Căn cứ tham khảo (có thể chưa đầy đủ):" followed by numbered provisions (document, locator, verbatim text) and a blank line.

Wording is developed only on synthetic prompts (no benchmark text) until final-label compliance reaches at least 95% on a synthetic set of at least 36 prompts, then frozen before any benchmark-item request. Alternative: tune S on the anchors; rejected as design on the evaluation set.

### 5. Scoring wrapper
B, O and K use the frozen chain on the raw response. S and KS take the response after the last final-answer label (the frozen label pattern). A multiple-choice response with no label is unparsed (empty prediction, incorrect, kept in the denominator) and the first-match parser is not applied to the whole response; reading uses the frozen last-label extraction unchanged. Each scored row adds `arm`, `format_ok` and `scoring_source` and keeps the raw text. Format compliance (label present; no markdown on the reading final line) is a separate summary field so a prompt-compliance loss is not mistaken for a reasoning loss. Alternative: a lenient fallback to the whole response; rejected because it reintroduces the first-match misread and would move S up or down for parser reasons.

### 6. Gates and registration order
`--execute` requires the card heading in `measurement_card.md` plus the detail card; endpoint host and model equal to the card; frozen manifest, template and evidence hashes; and, for `core`, `inference_ready`. The SHA-256 of `data/diagnostic_v1/protocol.json` binds the pilot, so the order is: (a) human review finished, imported and adjudicated so that `audit-report.json` is final; (b) controls resolved in `data/diagnostic_v1/readiness_controls_v1.json`, a file that names the protocol's SHA-256 and resolves exactly the controls the protocol leaves unset, so that `protocol.json` keeps its bytes (amended 2026-10-27: filling them into `protocol.json` would change its hash, and rebuilding the pilot to match would change the pilot content hash that every campaign manifest records, the frozen wide ones included); (c) cards registered and committed with title `pre-register MC-NN`; (d) `wide` may run as soon as (c) holds, `core` once `inference_ready` is true. Until (a) the decisions live in `preregistration-draft.json` in this change. Exception decided by the project owner on 2026-10-22: the wide card touches no audit anchor and needs none of the controls of `data/diagnostic_v1/protocol.json`, so it was registered first (MC-90, with its own entries in `registration.json`) while the review is still in progress; the core card follows steps (a) to (c). The campaign `protocol.json` and the prompt templates are frozen by the wide registration and change only through a new protocol version and new cards.

### 7. Evidence: oracle first, retriever second
Oracle evidence is the verbatim provisions the reviewer cites (recorded in the audit `evidence_reference`) for the evidence anchors of Decision 11, so it needs no retriever. Corpus v0 is the full text of the same documents plus a small set of distractor documents, chunked by article; retrieval is lexical (BM25 over articles) with a hashed configuration; O, K and KS run on the same anchors, so the gap between oracle and K isolates retrieval from evidence need. K and KS are a pilot on at most 12 items and their results are descriptive. Alternative: a corpus for all 146 Legal MC questions; rejected because the set spans many legal areas and only 24 questions name a numbered document, which is a separate project.

### 8. Analysis plan, fixed before data
The unit of analysis is the item. The outcome is the majority of correct replicates (core) or the single attempt (wide). Exact two-sided McNemar for each of S, O, K and KS versus B within the multiple-choice family and the reading family, Holm correction over the arm comparisons, Wilson intervals for proportions, passage-cluster bootstrap (2,000 resamples, seed 42) for reading differences; per-dataset results are descriptive only. The rule-based router is designed on wide, frozen before the first core request and evaluated once on core (Decision 11; the earlier order, tuned on core and evaluated once on wide, was dropped because the wide outcomes were unblinded first). Oracle-routing ceilings are reported next to the same ceiling over B replicates. Discordant pairs are exported in both directions for semantic review. Reference and secondary analyses were added before registration, after the MC-83 baseline showed a ceiling in the multiple-choice family and a strict exact-match scorer in reading: attempt-level rescue and regression between B replicates as the noise reference; a compliant-only sensitivity analysis; for reading, the paired token-F1 difference and a format-suspect flag on rescues; and, on wide only, a parity gate comparing B per dataset with the MC-83 interval. The multiple-choice family has only about thirty B-incorrect items on wide, so a null result there is reported as insufficient power, not as no effect.

### 9. Process-local endpoint resolution
The tunnel pushes no DNS, so the runner can map the endpoint host to the answer of the in-tunnel DNS server (`172.16.30.1`, documented in MC-88) inside its own process, leaving the `Host` header unchanged and the system resolver and hosts file untouched.

### 10. Registered supplements (added 2026-10-22, after the MC-90 results)
The wide run compared one S draw with one B draw per item, so its recovery and regression rates and its oracle ceiling mix the arm effect with sampling noise at temperature 1.0. A second B draw of the same items calibrates that. The frozen `protocol.json` is bound to MC-90 by hash (the manifest embeds its SHA-256), so a new phase cannot be added to it; a supplement instead re-runs arms of an existing phase under further replicate numbers and lives in the registration record: its own entry (base phase, arms, replicate numbers, card, manifest hash, operational limits, the keys of its analysis), its own card (MC-91 for `wide-b-r2`), manifest file and output directory, and the protocol and template hashes of the phase it supplements. Alternatives rejected: editing `protocol.json` (breaks the MC-90 registration), a second full protocol file (duplicates the frozen design), a command-line override of arms and replicates (nothing registered to check). The operational limits that proved necessary in MC-90 (at most 4 requests in flight, a breaker after 4 consecutive network failures) are registered in the supplement's condition instead of being an operator deviation. The noise analysis is `noise_reference`: it reads the second draw against the registered first draws and never replaces the MC-90 analysis. A second supplement, `wide-s-r2` (card MC-92), repeats arm S: MC-91 had shown that S changes more answers than a second B draw does, which cannot separate a systematic difference from a more variable arm. Its analysis `s_noise_reference` adds the noise of S itself, an instability comparison (exact McNemar on the per-item change indicators of the two arms), a sign test on the items one arm wins in both draws, the two-draw S-versus-B effect and the oracle ceiling over each arm's own two draws, with Holm over the four primary tests.

### 11. Router and evidence anchors for the core registration (amended 2026-10-26, before any core request)
The wide phase ran first (MC-90 to MC-92) and its outcomes per dataset are known: over two draws S adds 4.0 exact-match points in reading and nothing measurable in multiple choice. A router written now is informed by those outcomes, so wide can no longer test it. The roles are swapped: wide is the design set and core the evaluation set. Core has received no prospective request, has three replicates per arm and is the only phase with evidence arms.

- Rule. A router rule is a data file with a closed grammar: ordered conditions on three runtime features (the item's dataset, the length in characters of its frozen B prompt, case-insensitive literal cue words in its question text), each naming one deployable arm (B, S, K or KS), then a default arm. Any other key fails validation; gold answers, audit fields (diagnostic labels, gold validity, evidence sufficiency) and the outcomes of any arm are never features. O is a ceiling only, because reviewer-cited provisions do not exist at request time.
- Freeze. Two or three candidate rules are fitted on wide and the project owner chooses one. Its SHA-256 goes into the core registration entry and the core card, together with the best fixed arm (B or S, whichever has the higher mean accuracy over its two wide draws). `core --execute` refuses to start when the rule file is missing or its hash differs, and the evaluator refuses a rule whose hash differs from the registration, so the rule exists before any core outcome.
- Evaluation, once, on core. The item outcome is the strict majority of replicates, as in the core analysis. The evaluation set is the anchors whose core manifest contains the arm the rule selects for them; the rule and the manifest fix it before the run, and excluded anchors are listed. On that set the report gives, for the router, always-B, always-S and the best fixed arm, the accuracy with a Wilson interval and the mean reported total tokens per item; exact two-sided McNemar of the router against always-B and against always-S, Holm over the two; and the ceiling over the deployable arms available per item next to the ceiling over B replicates (the `oracle_routing` definition with O left out). A secondary block repeats the comparisons without anchors whose adjudicated gold is not valid. On wide the router is reported as design fit, without tests.
- Evidence anchors. Evidence arms run on the Legal MC anchors whose adjudicated input evidence is insufficient (the frozen eligibility rule) and whose adjudicated gold is valid. An insufficient anchor with suspect gold gets no evidence arm: if the cited provisions support another option than the gold, an evidence arm would measure the gold error, not the evidence effect; such anchors keep B and S and are listed separately. O, K and KS run on the same anchors. The list is derived from the adjudicated audit and first written into the core registration: the independent reviewer can read this repository and must not see the primary labels before finishing.
- K and KS join the first core registration if corpus v0 passes tasks 7.2 and 7.3 (no benchmark question, option, answer or model output in the corpus; provenance and hashes recorded; the share of cited articles in the top k measured once with the frozen configuration, misses listed, configuration unchanged afterwards). Otherwise core registers B, S and O, and K and KS follow under their own card. Corpus v0 is scoped by the documents the reviewer cited, so K measures article retrieval in a corpus known to hold the relevant documents, not open-domain retrieval.

Alternatives rejected: keeping wide as the evaluation set (its outcomes are known); a new held-out sample (core and wide use every item of the five datasets, and VMLU valid lies outside the rule's features); letting the router choose O (not deployable); evidence arms on every Legal MC anchor (the frozen protocol admits only insufficient input).

### 12. Diagnosis and routing (amended 2026-10-27, before any core request)
The audit's cause hypotheses exist only after an answer has been scored against the gold and reviewed, and only for the 60 anchors, which are the router's evaluation set; they can never be router features. They enter in four other places, so that each routing decision stays tied to the failure it is meant to address:

- Targets. Every condition and the default declare `targets`: the labels of the audit taxonomy (`data/diagnostic_v1/taxonomy.json`) that the routed arm is meant to address. The validator accepts only targets the arm can address: for S `reasoning_gap` and `reading_representation_gap` (S changes how the given input is processed, so it cannot separate the two), for K `external_evidence_gap`, `parametric_knowledge_gap` and `parametric_knowledge_error` (it adds provisions the input lacks), for KS their union, for B none; any arm other than B needs at least one target. Targets are declarations and never change which arm is selected. Cue words come from a lexicon grouped by need (calculation or combination of facts; legal provisions outside the input), written and hashed before any cue-level outcome on wide is computed (`data/diagnostic_prospective_v1/cue_lexicon_v1.json`). Each group names the labels its need points to, and a condition may test only cues of a group that shares one of its targets.
- Design fit with the semantic review. On wide, the design fit of a candidate also reports, for the reading items of the first draw pair, how many exact-match rescues and regressions in each routed group the semantic review of task 12 found to be measurement effects (the answer that lost on exact match was right in meaning), so that a condition chosen for a scoring artifact is visible before the owner picks a rule.
- Detection, on core. The router report adds a descriptive block over the evaluation set: among anchors whose adjudicated audit records at least one hypothesis, how many the router sent to an arm whose declared targets include one of them, in total and per label; among no-issue anchors, how many it sent to an arm other than B. The adjudicated labels come from a derived anchor-diagnosis file whose SHA-256 is recorded in the core registration; they score the routing and are never read by it.
- Recovery by hypothesis, on core. A separate registered analysis groups the anchors by adjudicated hypothesis (an anchor with two hypotheses appears in both groups), plus the groups no observed issue, unresolved, and issue observed with an empty hypothesis set, and reports per group the anchors whose B majority is incorrect and, per arm present, the rescues, regressions and anchors no arm answers correctly. It lists contrast candidates, anchor-hypothesis pairs whose matched contrast points the expected way: S against B for `reasoning_gap` and `reading_representation_gap` with sufficient input, O against B for `external_evidence_gap` with insufficient input, K failing where O rescues for `retrieval_gap`, and B replicates that disagree for `run_instability`. A candidate becomes `contrast_supported` only when a reviewer records it in the pilot's `contrasts.jsonl`, the existing path of the audit contract; the analysis never upgrades a hypothesis itself. The audited answers are the historical MC-72 ones, so an issue anchor whose new B majority is correct is reported as not reproduced.

Alternatives rejected: audit labels as router features (unknown at request time and present only on the evaluation set); a learned cause classifier (20 anchors carry a hypothesis, all of them at hypothesis level, and the two reviewers agreed on 0 of 3 hypothesis sets); a probe call, or a B-first cascade that escalates when two B draws disagree (deployable, but a new runtime component with its own cost and validation; future work).

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [S final-label non-compliance depresses S accuracy] → measure compliance separately, freeze the wording only after the synthetic-set threshold, report accuracy over all attempts.
- [Temperature 1.0 makes single draws noisy] → three replicates on core; wide relies on n, not replication.
- [Endpoint drift or a model swap behind the gateway] → record `response_model_id` per attempt, pause on a change, interleave arms in time, list drift under threats to validity.
- [A shared VPN profile may evict another user] → the operator connects and disconnects deliberately; runs are short.
- [Small K sample] → label it a pilot, use the oracle-first contrast, make no population claim.
- [Hash binding of `protocol.json`] → the registration order in Decision 6; nothing in this change edits that file.
- [Evidence leaks the answer] → the validator rejects letters and rationales; evidence is verbatim provisions.
- [Every item was answered by earlier runs and the anchors were audited] → core is unseen only for the prospective arms and conditions; audit fields are forbidden router features.
- [The router is fitted on wide outcomes] → it is evaluated only on core, its hash is frozen before the first core request, and wide figures are labelled design fit.
- [The router is evaluated on at most 60 items, and evidence arms exist on few of them] → exact tests and intervals; read as descriptive unless a corrected test is significant.
- [The cause hypotheses are few and unconfirmed] → detection and recovery by hypothesis are descriptive; candidates are listed for a reviewer, and no hypothesis is upgraded by the analysis.
- [Arm S cannot separate a reading failure from a reasoning failure] → both labels are S targets, and the report says that an S rescue supports the pair, not one of them.
- [Corpus v0 is scoped by the documents the reviewer cited] → declared in the card: K measures article retrieval in a corpus known to hold the relevant documents.
