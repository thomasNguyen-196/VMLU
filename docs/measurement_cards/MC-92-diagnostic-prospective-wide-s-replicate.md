# MC-92 — Qwen3.5-9B-65K (IEC), diagnostic prospective wide supplement: a second S draw on the 1,239 non-anchor items

Registered 2026-10-22 (+07) before benchmark-item requests of this card. The project owner asked for this run after the MC-91 results were reported. It does not change MC-90 or MC-91: their registered text, manifests and analyses stand, and this card only adds the missing noise measurement for arm S.

| Why | MC-91 measured the sampling noise of B and showed that S changes more answers than a second B draw does (reading: +8.5 points on the items B got wrong, +6.4 on those it got right). That comparison cannot say whether S differs systematically or is simply more variable, because its long reasoning chains at temperature 1.0 may make its own answers less stable. A second S draw measures that directly and gives every item two draws of each arm. |

| Analysis (fixed before data) | `s_noise_reference` in `code_benchmark/analyze_diagnostic_prospective.py`; its result keys are listed in the registration entry and checked by a test. Per family (multiple choice, reading): (1) drift between the two S draws, exact McNemar; (2) S-versus-S recovery (first draw wrong, second right) and regression rate with Wilson intervals, and the agreement rate; (3) **instability comparison** (primary test): the indicator "the result changes between the two draws" per item for B (MC-90 and MC-91) and for S; counts and rates, and an exact McNemar test on the items where only one arm changes; (4) **stable wins** (primary test): the items S gets right in both draws while B gets them wrong in both, against the reverse, exact sign test; Holm correction over these four primary tests (two families x two tests); (5) the two-draw effect: the mean over items of mean(S1, S2) minus mean(B1, B2), with a passage-cluster bootstrap interval (2,000 resamples, seed 42; one cluster per item for multiple choice); (6) the second S draw against each B draw: rescues, regressions, net gain and exact McNemar; (7) the oracle ceiling over B1 and S1 next to the ceilings over B1 and B2 and over S1 and S2; (8) mean token-F1 of the four draws and the mean absolute change between the two draws of each arm for reading; (9) per-dataset values, descriptive only; (10) final-answer-label compliance of the second S draw and the number of outputs cut at the token cap. Reading rule: S is called more variable than B in a family only if its Holm-adjusted instability p is below 0.05 with more S-only than B-only changes, and a systematic S effect in a family only if the Holm-adjusted stable-wins p is below 0.05 with more S wins; otherwise the result is reported as not distinguishable from noise. The analyses of MC-90 and MC-91 are neither repeated nor replaced. |
