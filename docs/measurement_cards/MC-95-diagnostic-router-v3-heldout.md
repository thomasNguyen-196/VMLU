# MC-95 — Qwen3.5-9B-65K (IEC), router v3 held-out test: B / S on 1,080 items no router has seen

Registered 2026-10-27 (+07) before benchmark-item requests of this card. The design is `openspec/changes/diagnostic-router-v3`. It does not change MC-90 to MC-94: their registered text, manifests, router v1 and v2 rules and analyses stand.

| Field | Condition |
| --- | --- |
| Why | The single evaluation of router v3. Router v2 (MC-94) beat always-B on VMLU valid but trailed always-S, because there S also helps where B's two answers agree. The wide draws and MC-94 show that whether S helps depends on the dataset: it helps even where B is consistent (VMLU, Vi-DROP), only where B's answers differ (Vi-SQuAD, Legal MC, ViBidLQA), or not at all (Legal NLI). Router v3 chooses the policy per dataset from a small labelled calibration sample instead of writing it by hand. |
| Router v3 | The calibration procedure `data/diagnostic_router_v3/procedure_v3.json`, SHA-256 `470d28a4ec7fb1df65f0e9f5d9afeed5cb7869ee04f0843b96b38d85b949c727` (`router-v3-calibrated`). For each dataset, on its 60 calibration items and in one rotation, it scores three candidates: always-B, the v2 cascade (B answers twice; the item goes to S when the two parsed answers differ under `normalized-answer-v1`) and always-S. Multiple choice is scored by accuracy, reading by mean token-F1 (exact match mostly rewards answer formatting, as the semantic review of MC-94 showed). The highest score wins; an exact tie goes to fewer mean reported tokens, then to the candidate order. The choices are written as a rule of the closed router grammar and routed by `diagnostic_router.route`. Exploratory check on the used draws (wide and MC-94; `analyze_diagnostic_router calibration-check`, 300 random 60-item samples per dataset): the choice lost 0.5 to 1.7 points on average against the best candidate on the other items. |
| Comparison rule | The frozen router v2 rule `data/diagnostic_router_v2/router_v2.json`, SHA-256 `0585b9a8980f6b906c2abdbcf69652ee7e8f1c51cca24697f9826d2e4ee26b3a`. It has no Vi-DROP item here, so on this set it is the pure cascade. |
| Scope | 1,080 items, each ranked by sha256(42, dataset:item); every dataset holds 60 calibration and 300 test items:<br>• VMLU valid outside MC-94: 360 of 444, by VMLU category in proportion.<br>• VM14K four-option items of the shuffled0 variant: 360 of 11,094, by category in proportion; rows with an empty question or placeholder options are left out.<br>• ViBidLQA train items whose question appears once across train, val and test: 360 of 1,848, at random.<br>Calibration takes the lowest-ranked items of each stratum, in proportion, and the manifest records them. No item was used to design or evaluate a router. Arms B and S, two draws each: 4,320 attempts (B 2,160, S 2,160). |

| Model / route / connectivity | As MC-90, MC-93 and MC-94 (see their cards): Qwen3.5-9B-65K served by the IEC internal gateway through the openvpn3 `member-shared-tcp` tunnel, direct API, OpenAI Responses API with streaming. Budget guard 5,000,000 reported tokens for this run (expected about 3 million). |
| Concurrency | Engine of 12 lanes (4 shards x 3 lanes) with at most 4 requests in flight and a breaker that pauses the run after 4 consecutive network failures. These limits are registered in `data/diagnostic_router_v3/registration.json` and applied by default; other values are refused. |
| Retries | No in-run retry of a failed item. `--resume` re-attempts only items without a completed response under an identical frozen condition; completed responses, including truncated or unparsed ones, are terminal. |
| Scoring | The scoring of MC-93 and MC-94: B on the raw response with the frozen V1 scorers; S from the text after the last final-answer label (`diagnostic-final-segment-v1`). VMLU and VM14K are scored by letter; ViBidLQA by exact match, with token-F1 kept. Unparsed answers stay in denominators. |
| Gates | Execution requires all of: the `MC-95` heading in `measurement_card.md`; this detail card; the registration with the held-out v3 protocol, campaign protocol, template and manifest hashes above; the endpoint host and model of the campaign; and `procedure_v3.json` and `router_v2.json` with their registered SHA-256. |
| Analysis (fixed before data) | `analyze_diagnostic_router heldout-v3`, plan `diagnostic-router-v3-heldout-v1`, run once.<br>• **Calibration.** Per rotation, the calibration items of each dataset choose its policy and the emitted rule routes the test items; calibration items are never test items.<br>• **Rotations.** Rotation 1 (first B draw answers, second B draw is the probe's second answer, first S draw on escalation) is primary. Rotation 2 (draws swapped) replicates it, calibration included. Item outcome: the single draw deployment would return.<br>• **Primary family.** Multiple choice (VMLU valid and VM14K, 600 test items). For v3, the frozen v2 rule, always-B and always-S: accuracy with a Wilson interval and mean reported tokens per test item, both probe answers included whenever the probe ran. Exact two-sided McNemar of v3 against v2 and against always-S on the pooled test items, Holm over the two. The confirmatory claim rests on this pair in rotation 1 only.<br>• **Per dataset and rotation.** The three calibration scores and the choice, the calibration cost in reported tokens, the best of always-B, v2 and always-S on the test items in hindsight, and the regret of v3 against it.<br>• **Reading (ViBidLQA train).** Descriptive: mean token-F1 and exact match per policy, plus the items whose exact-match correctness differs between v3 and a fixed policy, for an optional AI-assisted semantic review.<br>• The report's top-level keys are recorded in the registration and checked. |
| Provenance and outputs | `condition.json` (manifest, protocol, template, source, procedure, v2 rule, runner, engine, wrapper, scorer and card hashes, and the operational limits), per-attempt usage and response model id, and `operator_events.jsonl` for every launch, all under `all_res/diagnostic_router_v3/heldout/qwen3-5-9b-65k-prospective/`; no upload. |
| Comparison limits | • VMLU valid and VM14K items were answered by earlier benchmark runs (VM14K: MC-14 and MC-14b), and ViBidLQA train shares passages with the val and test splits, so the set is not pristine held-out.<br>• No Vi-DROP or Legal item is here, so v3's choices on those datasets rest only on the exploratory check.<br>• Exact match understates reading answers, which is why reading is descriptive.<br>• Single draws at temperature 1.0 are noisy; the second rotation bounds this, and a 60-item calibration sample can choose the wrong policy, which the per-dataset regret shows.<br>• The calibration cost (B twice and S once per calibration item) is paid once per dataset and reported apart from the per-item cost.<br>• The endpoint may have drifted since MC-94; the model-id check and B's VMLU accuracy against MC-94 can reveal it. |
