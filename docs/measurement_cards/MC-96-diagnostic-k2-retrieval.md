# MC-96 — Qwen3.5-9B-65K (IEC), K2 retrieval test: B / K / KS / O on 360 ViBidLQA questions without their passage

Registered 2026-10-27 (+07) before benchmark-item requests of this card. The design is `openspec/changes/diagnostic-k2-retrieval`. It does not change MC-90 to MC-95: their registered text, manifests, router v1 and v2 rules, the v3 procedure and analyses stand.

| Field | Condition |
| --- | --- |
| Why | Arm K has not yet helped. In MC-93, BM25 put the cited article among its three for 7 of the 8 Legal MC evidence anchors, yet the extra articles pulled the model off the answer, while the cited article alone (O) helped. K2 asks, on a task where the right evidence is known exactly, whether two retrieved passages recover what the question's own passage gives. It also asks whether the calibration idea of router v3 can decide whether to retrieve and how much to reason. |
| K2 procedure | `data/diagnostic_k2/procedure_k2.json`, SHA-256 `e7f2daa76d848f88e89fac6a850baef854839945df478380a04992c455caa961` (`router-k2-calibrated`). In one rotation, on the 60 calibration items, it scores four candidates by mean token-F1:<br>• always-B (closed book);<br>• always-K;<br>• the K cascade: K answers twice, and the item goes to KS when the two parsed answers differ under `normalized-answer-v1`, otherwise the first K answer stands;<br>• always-KS.<br>The highest score wins. Scores within 1e-12 count as equal, and a tie goes to the more thorough candidate, in the order B < K < K cascade < KS. The project owner chose this rule after MC-95, where a tie that went to the cheaper policy cost 7.3 points on VM14K. The choice is written as a rule of the closed router grammar, whose probe may now be arm K, and routed by `diagnostic_router.route`. The router reads only the closed-book request: the dataset, the B prompt and the question. |
| Retrieval | `data/diagnostic_k2/retrieval_k2.json`, SHA-256 `693916f23712f8a716908f492b70a89264fcc9303cc2fa1ce8c7796d8e512c0c`.<br>• Corpus: the 863 unique passages of the pinned ViBidLQA train, val and test sources (corpus SHA-256 `b501bdcbe1dd13fa4fb93ef82ae8c67894342a95d73dc2387a96b6504bbbca72`).<br>• Query: the question.<br>• Scoring: the corpus v0 tokenizer with bigrams and BM25 (k1 1.2, b 0.75).<br>• Kept: the top 2 passages, joined by a blank line.<br>It was chosen by the project owner after the exploratory K2.1 bake-off on the 1,928 train questions. That bake-off measured retrieval only: own passage at rank 1 for 64.7%, in the top 2 for 78.1%. BGE-M3 scored 49.2% at rank 1, a hybrid 60.8%, and a reranker added about 3 points at about 24 s per question on CPU. In this set, the own passage is at rank 1 for 184 and in the top 2 for 231 of the 300 test items; for the 60 calibration items, 40 and 46. The manifest records every item's two passages, scores and own-passage rank. |
| Scope | 360 ViBidLQA train questions that appear once across the splits and are not among the 360 of MC-95 (pool 1,488). They are asked as dataset `bidlqa-train-retrieval` and ranked by sha256(42, dataset:item); the 60 lowest-ranked are the calibration items and the next 300 the test items. Arms B, K, KS and O, two draws each: 2,880 attempts (720 per arm). |
| Prompts | • B, closed book: `Trả lời câu hỏi sau bằng một cụm từ hoặc số ngắn gọn.\n\nCâu hỏi: <question>\nTrả lời: `.<br>• K: the frozen main-v1 reading prompt (`build_reading_prompt`) with the two retrieved passages in the passage slot.<br>• KS: the frozen S reading prompt (`s_reading_head` of `prompt_templates_v1.json`, SHA-256 `9bcada89…`) on the same passages.<br>• O: the frozen reading prompt with the item's own passage, which is the ViBidLQA prompt of MC-95; O is a ceiling and is never deployable.<br>No prompt carries the gold answer. |
| Protocol and inputs | `data/diagnostic_k2/protocol.json`, SHA-256 `a8c5d481739dace26b88f7b5c36086315f5c07f471944f2da4bde7b63fb8ccdc`. Its model, transport, scoring and budget blocks are those of the campaign protocol (SHA-256 `fe1b1b89d19a93d74dc4b364c64a3308f0c7b6601cf61e90a69533238d80e137`, checked by the loader). Pinned sources: ViBidLQA train `f4b8c1ce…`, val `4cafca9d…`, test `b99b9484…`. The MC-95 manifest is excluded by file hash `f81f71ec…`. Manifest `heldout_k2.json`, self-hash `366bf4495edbb68e2997c58b1bea4fde76a29e52dda8fd3e795c296d37c808c2`, run identity `dataset:item:<ARM>-r<k>`. Send no gold. |
| Model / route / connectivity | As MC-90 and MC-93 to MC-95 (see their cards): Qwen3.5-9B-65K served by the IEC internal gateway through the openvpn3 `member-shared-tcp` tunnel, direct API, OpenAI Responses API with streaming. The endpoint host is resolved inside the runner process through the tunnel DNS. The endpoint self-reports `/home/llm/llama.cpp/models/Qwen3.5-9B-Q4_K_M.gguf`; a change of that id pauses the run. |
| Sampling and limits | As MC-95: temperature 1.0 requested, effective value not observable; seed and reasoning field omitted; system prompt `Answer the user's question.`; `max_output_tokens=4096`; 300 s timeout; no SDK retries. Budget guard 5,000,000 reported tokens for this run (expected about 3 million). |
| Concurrency | Engine of 12 lanes (4 shards x 3 lanes) with at most 4 requests in flight and a breaker that pauses the run after 4 consecutive network failures. These limits are registered in `data/diagnostic_k2/registration.json` and applied by default; other values are refused. |
| Retries | No in-run retry of a failed item. `--resume` re-attempts only items without a completed response under an identical frozen condition; completed responses, including truncated or unparsed ones, are terminal. |
| Scoring | The scoring of MC-93 to MC-95: B, K and O on the raw response with the frozen V1 reading scorer; KS from the text after the last final-answer label (`diagnostic-final-segment-v1`). Exact match and token-F1 are kept. Unparsed answers stay in denominators. |
| Gates | Execution requires all of: the `MC-96` heading in `measurement_card.md`; this detail card; the registration with the K2 protocol, campaign protocol, template, retrieval configuration and manifest hashes above; the endpoint host and model of the campaign; and `procedure_k2.json` with its registered SHA-256. |
| Analysis (fixed before data) | `analyze_diagnostic_router k2`, plan `diagnostic-k2-heldout-v1`, run once.<br>• **Rotations.** Rotation 1 (primary) uses the first draws of B, K, KS and O, with the second K draw as the probe's second answer. Rotation 2 (replication) swaps the draws. In each rotation the calibration items choose and the emitted rule routes the 300 test items; calibration items are never test items.<br>• **Primary test.** In rotation 1, the router against always-K and against always-KS on per-item token-F1: two-sided paired sign-flip tests with 20,000 Monte Carlo resamples (seed 7), Holm over the two. The confirmatory claim rests on this pair.<br>• **Per policy** (the router, the four candidates and O): mean token-F1 with a 95% percentile bootstrap (2,000 resamples, seed 7); exact match with a Wilson interval; mean reported tokens per test item, both K answers included whenever the probe ran. Exact-match McNemar counts are descriptive.<br>• **Description.** Per rotation, the calibration scores, choice and cost (B, K twice and KS per calibration item) and the regret against the best candidate in hindsight; retrieval hits; B, K, KS and O token-F1 on the items whose own passage was retrieved and on the others; the items whose exact match differs between the router and always-K or always-KS, for an optional AI-assisted semantic review.<br>• The report's top-level keys are recorded in the registration and checked. |
| Provenance and outputs | `condition.json` (manifest, protocol, template, retrieval configuration, corpus, source, excluded manifest, procedure, K2 module, runner, engine, wrapper, scorer and card hashes, and the operational limits), per-attempt usage and response model id, and `operator_events.jsonl` for every launch, all under `all_res/diagnostic_k2/heldout/qwen3-5-9b-65k-prospective/`; no upload. |
| Comparison limits | • ViBidLQA train items share passages with each other and with the val and test splits, and calibration and test items can share a passage, so the set is not pristine held-out.<br>• The K2.1 bake-off that chose the retriever ran on all train questions, including these items, though it measured retrieval only, never an answer.<br>• Closed-book B is expected near zero, so always-B is a floor, not a contender.<br>• Under token-F1 exact ties are rare, so the tie rule matters mainly when two candidates return the same answers.<br>• The K cascade escalates often on reading, since B pairs disagreed on about 58% of ViBidLQA items in the wide phase, and may cost more than always-KS; its tokens are reported.<br>• Exact match understates reading answers, which is why token-F1 is the score.<br>• Single draws at temperature 1.0 are noisy, and rotation 2 bounds this.<br>• The endpoint may have drifted since MC-95. The model-id check and O, which repeats MC-95's ViBidLQA B condition on new items, can reveal it. |
